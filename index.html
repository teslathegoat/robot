About

Security Researcher & Lifelong Learner

My journey into cybersecurity began around 2022, although I can't pinpoint the exact moment or reason that drew me in. It was probably a technical write-up, an unfamiliar vulnerability, or a problem that made me curious enough to keep digging.

What kept me hooked was the process itself: encountering something I didn't understand, spending hours exploring it, questioning assumptions, connecting seemingly unrelated pieces, and eventually uncovering how things worked beneath the surface. That curiosity gradually became more than an interest; it became the way I approach learning.

I've never been particularly good at limiting myself to a single discipline. I'm fascinated by people who think across boundaries, challenge conventional wisdom, and pursue ideas beyond what they're already familiar with. I've long admired thinkers and builders such as Bill Gates, Elon Musk, John D. Rockefeller, and Richard Feynman.

The philosophy of the early hacker culture resonates deeply with me: curiosity over credentials, understanding over assumptions, and the freedom to question how things are built rather than simply accepting how they work.

Over time, my understanding of cybersecurity has evolved. I've come to see that it's about much more than finding vulnerabilities in software. It's about understanding complex systems, the boundaries of trust, human behaviour, incentives, and the assumptions that hold everything together. Often, the most interesting weaknesses aren't in the code itself, but in the gaps between how a system is designed to work and how it actually behaves.

I'm driven by the desire to understand things at their deepest level, not just to learn how to use them, but to understand why they exist, how they work, and where they might break.
