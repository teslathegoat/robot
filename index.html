About

I study how large-scale statistical methods can be made reliable enough to trust — sparse where possible, private where necessary, and honest about their uncertainty everywhere else.

I am a senior research fellow at the Institute for Advanced Computation in Cambridge. My work sits between machine-learning theory and its practical use: I care less about the last percent of benchmark accuracy than about whether a method behaves predictably in the hands of a clinician, a census bureau, or a tired graduate student at two in the morning.

Before the institute I was a postdoctoral researcher at ETH Zürich; I did my doctorate at Cambridge. I teach one seminar each term, review for a small number of journals I actually read, and try to keep every published result reproducible from the raw data onward. The full text of my current work is on this page — no paywalls, nothing to download.
